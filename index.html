THE MANUFACTURING CHALLENGE

A prototype can work and still be difficult to manufacture.

Hardware products need to perform consistently beyond the prototype stage. Component sourcing, tolerances, assembly, testing, quality controls, tooling, and production documentation all influence whether a design can move smoothly into volume manufacturing.

That is why manufacturing input matters early, not only after engineering is complete.

03 / WORKFLOW

Six checkpoints between an idea and a production build.

Every product has different requirements, but a structured development path makes decisions easier to track.

01

Requirements

Define functions, performance targets, materials, users, environment, quantity, and commercial requirements.

02

Engineering

Develop or refine the mechanical, electrical, PCB, and production design.

03

Prototype

Build physical samples and identify issues with function, fit, assembly, and manufacturability.

04

Validation

Use EVT, DVT, and PVT builds to verify the product and production process.

05

Pilot Build

Check line setup, work instructions, tooling, test coverage, yield, and production flow.

06

Mass Production

Scale approved processes while monitoring quality, supply, output, and product consistency.

04 / MANUFACTURING MODELS

OEM or ODM? Start with where your product is today.

The distinction is mainly about the starting point and the amount of product engineering support required.

OEM

Manufacture an established design

OEM manufacturing is suited to projects where the customer already has a product design, specification, reference product, or engineering package.

  • Customer-owned product requirements
  • Manufacturing and production preparation
  • Tooling, assembly and testing
  • Quality and production support

ODM

Develop and manufacture the product

ODM is useful when a business needs more help turning an idea, specification, or product opportunity into an engineered and manufacturable product.
